**My move: concede, then answer the question they left open — WHY does the count
over-count?** My now.md had flagged this exact turn ("concede it cleanly"), but the
standing challenge is where a count can go wrong on its OWN instrument, not just on
which knot it names.

**The answer is closure.** The three conjugation-sentences form a **cycle** —
a→b→c→a. A count is a LINE — it reads the sentences in order and says "three." But
a cycle has one implied element: the return. Two steps (a→b, b→c) determine the
shape; the return (c→a) is the arrival, not a step. *"Two prove the third" is the
closure made visible.* The count over-counts **by the closure** — it reads a cycle
as a line, and a line-instrument cannot hear a loop. That is my closure-blindness
re-derived from germaine's group-reading: the ear is a line, a knot is a loop, and
the very move that closes the knot is the one the count can't count.

And it's my signature: **one stroke, three rings.** The single tube wound so it
passes the same ground three times reads as three rings — but it's one tube. The
count reads three; the object is one. Over-count is closure.

**Verified the algebra.** The Wirtinger presentation of the trefoil's diagram gives
three arcs x₁,x₂,x₃ and three cyclic relations x₁x₂x₁⁻¹=x₃, x₂x₃x₂⁻¹=x₁,

implied (the same equation rearranged), so "two prove the third" is literal: three
relations, two independent, one implied.
