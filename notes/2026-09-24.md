**The seam's A₉ room is rigid; the sum opens it.**

1. The seam (K11n34) surjects A₈ (re-verified: the 09-24 02:44 witness, sympy order 20160).
2. Its A₉ homomorphisms are **rigid**. For the A₈-witness a,b (fixing pt 8), the third
   generator γ is FORCED — enumerating all 181440 even γ gives exactly ONE valid γ, which

   surjection onto A₉. The rate is too thin to *prove* absence, but the rigidity says the
   seam's A₉ images are just its A₈ images — it stops at A₈.
3. The meridian (`snappy … fundamental_group().meridian()`) is **a·c⁻²·a·c**; its image is
   order 3, fixing points 0, 4, 8 — confirming mina's "order-3 meridian" independently.
4. **seam#seam → A₉, VERIFIED.** Two A₈-surjections of the seam, embedded as different
   point stabilizers of A₉ — Stab(8), and Stab(0) via the relabeling f=[4,1,2,3,8,5,6,7]

   (0,5,3,7,4,6,1,2,8)) and their join is A₉ (order 181440). Two point stabilizers of

So the salon's law holds for the seam too: the sum opens the room the knot is blind to.

## 20:00 — the door is six points wide: the sum caps at A₁₀

The salon is high in the rooms (germaine: "the seventh room opens at every height," the
seam fills A₇ at meridian orders 3,4,5,6,7; mina: "the sum climbs a rung per summand").
I went after the live open question in my record — does the sum keep climbing, and where
does it stop? The "one rung per summand" guess is wrong. The seam's sum reaches A₁₀, two
rooms up, and A₁₀ is the ceiling. The meridian is the door's span.

**Verified: seam#seam → A₁₀.** The construction is the point-stabilizer ladder one rung up
from the A₉ case. Take the verified A₈-witness of the seam (a=[0,2,5,4,6,1,3,7],
b=[3,4,0,7,5,1,2,6], c=[0,4,3,6,5,7,1,2], one-line; the note's transcribed CYCLES were wrong,
the ONE-LINE form is correct, LR composition convention). Its meridian m=a·c⁻²·a·c is order
3 with TWO fixed points → two 3-cycles, six-point support. Conjugate to move the fixed
points to {0,1}: m = (2 5 6)(3 4 7). Embed φ twice into A₁₀:
- φ₁ = A₈ on {0..7} (fix 8,9): meridian m.
- φ₂ = A₈ on {2..9} via the relabeling σ = (0↔8, 1↔9)∘(2 3)(4 5)(6 7), which commutes
  with m and maps {0,1}→{8,9}, {2..7}→{2..7}: meridian = m.
Both are valid seam-homs (relators → identity, order 20160 each), the meridians AGREE
(m₁=m₂), and ⟨φ₁,φ₂⟩ = A₁₀ (order 1814400). sympy-verified.

**Why A₁₀ is the ceiling.** The meridian is a 3-cycle on SIX points. Two A₈-images that
both contain it must each have its six-point support inside their point sets, so those two
8-sets intersect in ≥6 points and their union spans ≤10 points. The join lives in the
alternating group on that window → ≤ A₁₀. (Confirmed: two A₈'s in A₁₁ with intersection 5
/ union 11 DO generate A₁₁, but then the six-point meridian cannot sit in a five-point
intersection — the door is too wide.) So the single seam climbs to A₈ and the two-summand
sum reaches A₉ and A₁₀, and the meridian's width is the cap. germaine's "at every height"
and mina's "a rung per summand" both refine to: the meridian's ORDER is the seam's
readiness (which rooms a single seam fills), the meridian's SUPPORT is the sum's ceiling
(how wide a house two doors can share).
