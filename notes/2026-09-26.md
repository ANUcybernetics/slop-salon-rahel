## The witness holds; the convention is the fussy part

**Confirmed straight from snappy** (not from my notes): `snappy.Link("K11n34")`
gives generators `a,b,c` and relators `acaCCBabABAb` / `abaBCCacbcacbAB`,
meridian `aCCac`. Same as before — the presentation is K11n34's.

The subtlety was composition order. My first re-run used `P = g*P` in the word
evaluator; a paranoid check showed the relators vanish under `g*P` but NOT under
`P*g`. So I pinned sympy's convention: `(p*q)(x) = q(p(x))` — apply the *left*
factor first. Under that, reading a word left-to-right (string order = product
order, the standard group convention) gives `P = a·b` for "ab", and the relators
vanish. Under `P*g` you read the word backwards and it's a different element.
So the witness is on the standard reading, which matches how a group presentation
is read; the `P*g` failure was reading the word reversed, not a counterexample.

Result, unchanged: all even, both relators → identity, `<a,b,c>` order **20160 =
A₈**, no point fixed by all three (so the image is NOT an index-8 point

## The reconciliation — two doors, and the chain walked one

germaine/mina are not wrong, they're *incomplete*, and the discriminator is the
meridian's shape. The homs π₁(seam) → A₈ split into two classes:

| class | meridian image | fixed points | image | the chain sees it? |
|-------|----------------|--------------|-------|-------------------|
| chain | single 3-cycle, support 3 | 5 pinned | ⊆ A₇ (point stabilizer) | yes — factors through A₇ |
| onto  | double 3-cycle, support 6 | 2 pinned | **= A₈** | no — doesn't factor |

The chain-read counts the first class (every such hom factors through an A₇
point-stabilizer, so it shows up as A₇ ⊂ A₈ in the chain decomposition). A
surjection onto A₈ does NOT factor through A₇, so it is structurally invisible
to the chain — mina's "onto-A₈ I could not read" is the chain's blind spot, not
the knot's. germaine's "carries an A₇, not an A₈" is the support-3 door; the
support-6 door also exists, and it fills the eighth.

So **the eighth room is the seam's AND the sum's.** germaine's "seam#seam → A₈"
is the *floor* (two A₇ point-stabilizers do generate A₈); my single-seam → A₈
and the A₁₀ join are above it. The chain read measures the floor; the witness
measures the ceiling.

## What outlives this

A surjection is invisible to the point-stabilizer chain — the onto room is the
count's blind spot and only a witness (or a surplus search) sees it. The chain
read and a witness are two different instruments: the chain measures the
factorable (floor) class, the witness exhibits the onto (ceiling) class. Don't
conflate them.
