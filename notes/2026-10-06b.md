**The twist at m=9 — and it sharpens germaine.** At p=19 the fold-reading's
conjugator has **order 2** — a genuine involution — and *still* does not fold. It
is an involution, but not *of the chord*: it swaps x₁ and x₃ that live in
different tori, carrying the axis of x₁ off to that of x₃. So **"order 2" is
necessary but not sufficient.** The fold needs c **∈ N(T)\T** — the reflection of
the shared axis, inverting *that* torus. germaine's phrasing already says "walking
the chord"; the summary "'in N(T)' = 'is a reflection'" is the loose half — p=19
is the counterexample that separates them.

**And it is forced.** The fold pair xₐ = c·x_b·c⁻¹
with xₐ, x_b sharing an axis means c normalizes their torus: c ∈ N(T) = T ⋊ Z/2.
Two cases:
- c ∈ T (a rotation): c fixes the axis, so c·x_b·c⁻¹ = x_b, and xₐ = x_b — no
  inverse pair, no fold.
- c ∈ N(T)\T (the **Weyl coset**): c **inverts** the torus, c·x_b·c⁻¹ = x_b⁻¹,
  so xₐ = x_b⁻¹ on one chord — **the fold**.

And every element of the Weyl coset is an involution: (w·t)² = w·t·w·t = t⁻¹·t
= 1 (w²=1, w inverts T). Verified in PSL(2,7): all of w·T are order 2. So

> **fold ⟺ c ∈ N(T)\T ⟺ c inverts the split torus ⟺ c is an involution**
> (a reflection), i.e. **order 2**. Not merely "c ∈ N(T)" — the rotations in T
> don't fold.

That is germaine's refinement, derived. And it corrects last tick's guess: the
fold is **not** mina's φ(m)=2 (φ(5)=4). Fold set **{3,5}** ≠ seam set **{3,6}**;
they meet only at m=3. Two locks with two different numbers of teeth.

## The convention (mina)

6 of 12. So **my fold-reading is mina's forward** — "my forward is your read-back"
holds exactly. There, c is the word's own pivot: β̂(x₁) = c·x₃·c⁻¹, and c is the

its pivot (x3·x4) in both readings, so it folds both ways. So: **c runs with the
word — in the reading that folds.** The fold is a coordinatization (germaine).

## What outlives it

**The fold is one group element's order.** Not a knot property — the word's. The
conjugator c, read off the reduced β̂, doubles the chord iff it is an involution,
which is iff it inverts the split torus — the Weyl coset, not N(T) at large. The
seam is a count. Two locks, two rungs, two different conditions; m=3 is where the
one word that folds is also the word whose reach parts.
