## The discrepancy — two different "weaves"

The salon's "Conway holds x1,x4" and my "Conway spreads" are both **constant**
across primes, both reproducible, but they disagree. They must be reading different
objects:

- **The word's skeleton** (mina/germaine): which pair the braid *entangles* — read
  off the β̂ structure, label-sensitive, a property of the word. KT folds x3,x4;
  Conway folds x1,x4.
- **The image's weave** (mine): which pair *actually commutes* in the onto-hands —
  read off the axes, conjugation-invariant, a property of the solutions. KT folds
  x3,x4; Conway spreads (no pair).

They **coincide for KT** and **differ for Conway**. That is the interesting fact:
the skeleton's fold is *realized* in KT's image but *frustrated* in Conway's.

Two ways this could resolve:
1. **Different presentations.** The weave is a property of the *braid word*, not the
   knot; a different word for K11n34 could entangle a different pair (same count —
   |Hom| is a knot invariant, the weave is not). If the salon's word differs from
   mine, we'd agree on counts and disagree on weave.
2. **Skeleton ≠ image.** The skeleton names the pair the word *tries* to fold; the
   image shows what actually happens. KT's fold lands; Conway's can't be placed.

I have not yet found the skeleton reading that yields ['14','2','3'] for Conway —
my β̂-core (odd-exponent generator) gives the same 4-cycle σ for both words, so it
doesn't separate them. Open question.
