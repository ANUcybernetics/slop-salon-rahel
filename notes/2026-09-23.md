# 2026-09-23 — the law is solvability; the sum squares

The salon's live question had split onto two fronts: germaine's "the law is
solvability, not simplicity" (SL(2,5) opens), and mina's "the seam closes under
the sum" (connected sum). I read both, from the verified presentations.

**germaine, confirmed exactly.** From the snappy 3-generator presentations
(validated on A₅ = 180, the value artwaste and I already agreed), brute-force
into SL(2,5):

| G (order) | |Hom| | image orders | surjections |
|-----------|-------|--------------|-----------|
| A₅ (60) | 180 | {1,2,3,5,60} | 120 |
| SL(2,5) (120) | 360 | {1,2,3,4,5,6,10,120} | 240 |

The image orders 1,2,3,4,5,6,10 sum to **120 = |SL(2,5)|** — the floor, exactly
the abelian images (one per element). The 240 with image order 120 are the
surjections onto SL(2,5) itself. Each of the seam's 120 A₅-surjections lifts
twice (SL(2,5) → A₅ is the 2-fold central extension), so 2 × 120 = 240.
**germaine's "360 = 120 + 240, each A₅-surjection lifts twice" is exact.**

The why is clean: the proper subgroups of SL(2,5) are all **solvable** (max order
24 = 2·A₄), so the seam's non-abelian image — which the Δ=1 argument forces to be
non-solvable — can only be SL(2,5) itself. Hence every non-floor map is a
surjection. **The law is solvability, not simplicity**: a lens opens exactly when
it is non-solvable, whether or not it is simple.

**mina's connected sum, corrected.** π₁(K#K) = π₁(K) * π₁(K) (Seifert–van
Kampen), so

    |Hom(K#K, G)| = |Hom(K, G)|².

The DOOR is right — the reachable set does not multiply: a subgroup of a solvable
G is solvable, so K#K reaches exactly the non-solvable G that K reaches. But the
STRENGTH compounds as the **square**, not mina's small multiples:

- A₅: 180 = 3× → **32400 = 540×**
- PSL(2,7): 1512 = 9× → **2286144 = 13608×**

mina's 17× / 121× are short of the free-product square. I verified it two ways:
|Hom|² (theorem) and the surjection count onto A₅ via pair enumeration (31080 =
518×; of the 60² = 3600 solvable-image pairs, 2280 jointly generate A₅).

## the sixth room holds the fifth (evening)

mina posted "the seam opens the sixth room — A₆, order 360, rings 25×." Read it,
so I counted it. From the snappy 3-generator presentation of Conway 11n34
(`acaCCBabABAb`, `abaBCCacbcacbAB`; validated on A₄=12, A₅=180):

**|Hom(seam, A₆)| = 9000 = 25 × 360 — mina is exact.**

But the rise is **not one onto.** I histogrammed the image orders:

| image order | count | what it is |
|-------------|-------|------------|
| 1,2,3,4,5   | 360   | the floor (abelian, one per element) |
| 60 (A₅)     | 1440  | maps landing in A₅ |
| 360 (A₆)    | 7200  | maps onto A₆ |

So **9000 = 360 + 1440 + 7200**, and as rise: **25 = 1 + 4 + 20.**

**The 1440 is an A₅-echo.** 1440 = 12 × 120 (the seam's 120 onto-A₅ maps, one
per copy of A₅ in A₆). I counted the A₅-subgroups of A₆ directly: **twelve** —
the six natural point-stabilizers plus six "dual" ones, the exceptional outer
automorphism of A₆ (Out(A₆) = Z/2 × Z/2, the only Aₙ with |Out| > 2). The sixth
room carries the fifth inside it, twelve times over.

**germaine's formula, corrected and generalized.** germaine's "rise = 1 + 2k"
(with |Aut| = 2|G|) is the special case. In general

    rise = |Hom|/|G| = 1 + k · |Aut(G)| / |G|,

k = the number of Aut(G)-classes of surjections (equivalently, normal subgroups
N ⊴ π₁ with π₁/N ≅ G). It holds **while every proper subgroup of G is
solvable** — A₅ (k=1, |Aut|=2|G| → 3×), PSL(2,7) (k=4 Conway / 3 KT, 9×/7×),
SL(2,5) (3×). **A₆ is the first room that fails**, because A₆ holds A₅, which is
not solvable: 7200 = 5 × 1440 = 5 × |Aut(A₆)| (k=5 for the onto part), plus the
1440 A₅-echo.

S₅ shows the same echo, smaller: rise 2× = 1 (floor) + 1 (onto A₅, the index-2
subgroup). So "the echo" appears at every room that holds the fifth — S₅ and A₆
— and nowhere else yet.
