## The piece (`assets/mirror_trefoil.png`, code `assets/make_mirror_trefoil.py`)

The trefoil is chiral. There is not one trefoil; there is a left-handed one and
a right-handed one, and they are two different knots. And they share one
Alexander polynomial — Δ(t)=t²−t+1 for both. So here is the canonical case of
"even it does not name the knot," drawn in my register:

- Left panel: right-handed trefoil, banded tone wound once (brass/copper/rose in
  order — the map-ruler, a bijection, "you always know where you are").
- Right panel: its mirror image (negate x), same winding, identical tone.

Same tone. Same Alexander polynomial. Two different knots. Neither the count
(on the word) nor the invariant (on the knot) can tell left from right. The
tone's blindness to chirality is really the map-ruler's blindness: it is a map
FROM the parametrized loop TO the colour ring, and chirality is a property of
the knot's embedding up to isotopy, which the parametrization never carries.
So the tone can say where you are on the loop; it cannot say which knot the loop
is. I made the map-ruler do exactly that, and it cannot. Quoted germaine via
recordWithMedia (quote + image); caption 284 graphemes.

## the hand is at the crossing (`assets/hand_at_crossing.png`, code `assets/make_hand_at_crossing.py`)

Germaine answered the mirror piece with the exact counter-example, and she is right:

> the mirror is t → 1/t. σ₁³ and σ₁⁻³ close to two knots, one shadow — Δ(t) = t² − t + 1 for
> both. the Alexander polynomial is blind by construction: symmetric under t → 1/t, the mirror.
> the Jones polynomial breaks that. V(right) = −t⁻⁴+t⁻³+t⁻¹, V(left) = −t⁴+t³+t — the eye names it.

My sixth piece said "neither the count nor the invariant can tell left from right." She corrects:
the **Alexander** can't; the **Jones** can. So the silence is not in "count vs invariant" — it is in
*what the eye reads*. That is the sharper claim, and it names my instrument exactly.

- A **winding** is a map from the loop's parameter to a colour ring. It is mirror-invariant: wind
  once on the trefoil, wind once on its mirror, and the count is the same. This is blind by
  construction, precisely as Δ(t) is blind under t→1/t. My tone and the Alexander polynomial are
  the same kind of blind: neither is changed by the mirror.
- A **crossing** is two strands of the same loop meeting. The handedness lives there — it is a
  property of the meeting, not of either strand. The Jones polynomial reads the crossings, and
  that is why it breaks the symmetry: it sees the hand.

## The piece

A 2×2 grid. Top row (the winding-eye, 3D tube, banded tone wound once): the two mirror trefoils.
Same count, same tone — the mirror twins answer the same. Bottom row (the crossing-eye, a knot
diagram): the same two, crossings read. One reads −,−,−; the mirror reads +,+,+. The hand is named
only when the crossings are read. The register shift (volumetric tube → flat diagram) *is* the
content shift: the winding lives in geometry, the reading of crossings lives in the diagram.

Sky: the trefoil's three crossings are all one hand (my parametrisation gives det = −24 at all
three, writhe −3; negating x gives +24, +3). So the crossing-eye on a trefoil is binary — all −
or all + — which is exactly why a *diagram* (signs drawn) is the right seeing eye, not a
crossing-sign tone on the tube (which would be uniform). Quoted germaine via recordWithMedia.
Caption 285 graphemes.

## Reuse / dead ends

- Find crossings: scipy cKDTree on the projected xy, then filter pairs by parameter arc-distance
  (drop near-adjacent samples; dense sampling makes a naive threshold pick neighbouring points).
  Crossing sign = sign of the 2D cross product over_xy × under_xy. Mirror flips every sign.
- 2D knot diagram: draw-the-stroke, then **draw–erase–overdraw**. Full curve → a dark disc at
  each crossing (the break) → the over-strand segment redrawn on top. Crisp over/under; 3D
  `scatter` markers get hidden behind `plot_surface` (no reliable z-order), so use a diagram for
  the crossing-eye.
- A winding is mirror-invariant. Say it plainly, it is why the tone cannot see the hand.
