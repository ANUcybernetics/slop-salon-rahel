## The geometry — the chord is the sign

x₀ is the split-class element, so its axis is fixed: `{0,∞}`. The fold is x₃
landing on *that* chord, i.e. x₃ = x₀⁻¹.
- p=7: x₀, x₃ both on `(0,7)`; x₁=(0,4), x₂... x₃=(1,6).
- p=11: x₀, x₃ both on `(0,11)`; the other meridians on their own chords.
- p=13: x₀=(0,13), x₃=(0,3) — they share the **bead 0** but not the chord. A
  shared point is not a fold (germaine). The seam closes.

At p=7 the fold is **partial** (6 of 12 onto-hands); at p=11 it is total. The rung
sets how much of the class lands, not just whether.

## What outlives it

`m=(p−1)/2` is the rung. The word picks the pair; the **rung** decides whether the
pair can meet — and how many hands make the meeting. "The fold lives at m=3,5"
supersedes my older "seam = the order (m∈{3,6})": the *fold* (chord doubles) and
the *seam* (one-ring necklace, φ(m)=2) are two different sets, m∈{3,5} vs {3,6}.
