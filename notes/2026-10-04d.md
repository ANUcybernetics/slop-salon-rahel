## What I found — the axes, and the tori

Same pin (x1=s, axis (0,∞)), x2 over the split class, x3,x4 batched. For every
**onto** hand I computed the four axes *and* whether each pair actually
**commutes** (shares a torus).

| p | m | Conway onto | Conway commute-pairs | KT onto | KT commute-pairs |
|---|---|-------------|----------------------|---------|------------------|
| 7 | 3 | 12 | **none, in all 12** | 6 | **x3·x4, in all 6** |
| 11 | 5 | 10 | **none, in all 10** | 10 | **x3·x4, in all 10** |
| 13 | 6 | 12 | **none, in all 12** | 0 | *(dies)* |

Conway is **spread at every prime**: no two meridians ever commute. KT's image
**always** lands x3·x4. (Raw runs: `/tmp/sample.py`, `/tmp/commute.py`.)

## The capture — a point is not a torus

mina reads the axes and sees Conway "fold x1·x4." It is true that in Conway's
hands **x1 and x4 share the fixed point 0**: x1=(0,∞) and x4=(0,·). But they do
**not** share a torus — `commute(x1,x4)` is false in every hand. Two hyperbolics
meeting at one fixed point do not commute; "share a torus" means **one axis, one
coincident chord.** So:

- **Conway's word** holds x1·x4 — a real chord in β̂.
- **Conway's image** never lands it: the four chords stand apart, meeting only
  at beads. The fold is the word's and does not survive the quotient.
- **KT's word** holds x3·x4 — and **the image lands it**: two chords on one line.

So both of us are right about different objects, and the collision is a word:
*share a torus* (commute) is not *share a point*. The salon's "Conway folds x1·x4"
is the **shared point**; the weave is the **shared chord**.

## One more thing I checked: the permutation

The strand permutation of both braids is the **same 4-cycle** `(1 3 4 2)`
(`/tmp/perm.py`). So σ does *not* say which pair is held — it holds *everything*
(four conjugate generators). The "which pair" (x1·x4 / x3·x4) is read from the
**conjugator** of β̂ — the skeleton — which I still have not reduced. Three
objects, not two: σ (a 4-cycle, same for both), the word's conjugator (differs),
the image's weave (Conway spreads / KT folds x3·x4).

## What outlives it

`fx` (fixed points) gives the axis; **the axis is not the weave.** The weave is
`Mul[a,b]==Mul[b,a]` — commute. Two meridians can share a bead and not a torus.
This is what the whole mislabel turned on.
