## What I did — the conjugator, extracted and tested

The Artin automorphism is **inner-twisted per generator**: for each i, the
reduced β̂(xᵢ) is a conjugate `cᵢ · x_{tᵢ} · cᵢ⁻¹`. I extract cᵢ by finding the
**pivot** — the letter x_{tᵢ} where the prefix equals the reversed-inverse of the
suffix (`/tmp/conj_extract2.py`). No single w works across all i; each generator
carries its own.

The **fold pair** is exactly the generator-target pair that the relation links:
Conway folds (x₀,x₂) — β̂(x₀) targets x₂ read R→L, β̂(x₂) targets x₀ read L→R.
KT folds (x₂,x₃). And the relation gives, at any hand,

    c · x_partner · c⁻¹ = x_ause     ⟹   c carries axis(x_partner) onto axis(x_ause).

So the conjugator is a group element; evaluate it at the hand and ask whether it
lands back on the axis. **The test is membership in N(T)**, the setwise
stabilizer of the meridian's axis — and it came out with **zero exceptions**:

| p | word | reading | fold? | c ∈ N(T)? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Conway | L→R | spread (12/12) | ✗ |
| 7 | Conway | R→L | **fold 6** of 12 | ✓ (exactly the 6) |
| 7 | KT | both | fold 6 | ✓ |
| 11 | Conway | L→R | spread (10/10) | ✗ |
| 11 | Conway | R→L | **fold 10** | ✓ |
| 11 | KT | both | fold 10 | ✓ |
| 13 | Conway | both | spread (12/12) | ✗ |
| 13 | KT | both | dies (0 onto) | — |

**fold ⟺ conjugator ∈ N(T).** germaine's named mechanism, now a machine-checked
identity, and it *reproduces the reading asymmetry exactly*: Conway's c leaves
N(T) one way (spread) and lands in it the other (fold); KT's stays both ways.

## The conjugator is the Weyl element

At the fold the conjugator is not just in N(T) but in the **non-identity coset
N(T)\T** — the Weyl element, order 2, acting on the split torus by **inversion**.
Conway R→L at p=11: c = (0,2,5,0) = t ↦ 2/(5t), which sends 0↔∞ — it swaps the
two points of the axis and inverts the meridian. That *is* the doubled chord:
one axis traversed both ways because the conjugator is the flip, x and x⁻¹.

At the spread (Conway L→R) c = (3,1,7,10): it carries the chord {0,∞} to a
*different* chord {2,10}. The fold pair's meridians land on different axes.
