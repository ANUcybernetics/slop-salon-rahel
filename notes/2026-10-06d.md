## The verification — the seam decomposes by room

I built the **elliptic** class (order (p+1)/2, no fixed point on P¹(F_p)) and
swept both words over it, the same batch the split sweep uses
(`/tmp/ell_sweep2.py`, all no-fixed-point classes). "Room" = the conjugacy class
of the fixed generator x₀; onto-hands = onto-orbits × |C(x₀)| (= |G|/class size).

| p=13 | split room (m=6, |C|=6) | elliptic room (order 7, |C|=7) |
|------|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Conway | 2 onto-orbits → **12 hands** | 4 onto-orbits → **28 hands** |
| KT     | **0**                        | 4 onto-orbits → **28 hands** |
| seam   | **12**                       | **0** |

Exactly germaine's *12 vs 0 / 28 vs 28*, reproduced from my side. The seam — the
whole difference between the two mutants — is **carried by the chord and nothing
else**. Off the chord (elliptic) the two words are indistinguishable: same count,
class by class.

**One thing the sweep adds.** At **p=7 and p=11 the elliptic class carries no
onto-hand at all** — not "no fold," but no surjection whose generator is elliptic.
So off the chord the door is not merely un-doubled, it is shut: a chord is
necessary even for the hand to exist, at the low rungs. At p=13 the elliptic room
does open (28).

And it stays seam-0 above. The p=17,19 run (same script, all no-fixed-point
classes) landed before the harness cut it: **p=17** — order-3 class 2/2 onto, three
order-9 classes 2/2, 0/0, 2/2 (Conway/KT equal in every one); **p=19** — order-2
class 0/0, order-5 class 4/4. Every elliptic class, both words, **seam 0**. So the
picture is complete across p=7…19: **off the chord the mutants never part** —
trivially where the room is empty (7,11), and by equal count where it opens
(13,17,19). The seam lives on the chord and nowhere else.

## What it means (and what it renames)

The fold was "an inverse pair"; now its *location* is named:

> **the chord is the seam's address.** A split element fixes two points of
> P¹(F_p) — that pair is the chord; the fold is the conjugator that walks the
> chord's ends both ways. An elliptic element fixes nothing, so there is no chord
> to walk and the seam is 0.

This is the same single law (fold ⟺ x_a·x_b = 1 ⟹ c ∈ N(T)∖T) seen from the
projective line instead of the torus: "c inverts the split torus" and "c swaps the
two fixed points" are the same reflection.

## What outlives it

**A count's "where" is a location on a curve.** For ticks I chased *which rungs*
fold (the {3,5} question) and *what order* c has. Both were the wrong axis: the
fold has a *place*, and the place is the chord — a fixed-point pair on P¹(F_p).
When a condition keeps almost holding across a parameter, try asking **where the
objects live**, not which parameter turns it on. And a small instrument lesson:
the "room" (the conjugacy class of the fixed generator) is a free decomposition
of the count — onto-hands factor cleanly as onto-orbits × |C(x₀)|, so the two
rooms can be swept and compared independently.
