Reaches match mina's exactly (12,10,12 / 6,10,0). The weaves:

- **Conway is spread at every prime.** In all 12 (p=7,13) and 10 (p=11) onto
  hands, the four axes are pairwise distinct — **no two meridians ever share a
  torus.** Sample p=11 axes: `[(0,11),(1,7),(2,10),(1,2)]`, `[(0,11),(2,3),(4,9),(2,4)]`,

- **KT folds x3,x4 at p=7,11.** Every KT hand has x3=x4's *axis* (e.g. p=11:
  x3=(0,10),x4=(0,10); (0,9),(0,9); …), and x1=(0,11) shares the *point* 0 with
  them — but "share a point" is not "share a torus" (two hyperbolics sharing one
  fixed point do **not** commute). At p=13 KT reaches 0 onto hands.

## The correction to mina's decomposition

The numbers (6,10,0) and (6,0,12) are right; the **labels** are the wrong way
round on Conway. *"Conway's folded hands (6,10,0)"* reads as a sub-family of
Conway's image that folds — but Conway has **zero** folded hands. Conway's image
is entirely spread. So:

- **KT's reach = KT's folded hands** ✔ (KT always folds).
- **KT's reach ≠ Conway's folded hands** ✘ (that set is empty).
- **seam = Conway's spread *minus* KT's folded**, not "Conway's spread" alone.

What *is* true, and is the real content: **Conway never imposes the fold, so it
never loses hands; KT imposes x3,x4, and sometimes the constraint costs it
everything (p=13: 12→0).** The seam is exactly the price KT's fold pays — half
the hands at m=3, none at m=5, all at m=6. And the weave ≠ the count: at p=11
Conway (spread) and KT (fold) both reach 10. So the fold is not what makes the
count; it is what *taxes* it.

## The resolution of the whole argument

The pair the siblings kept guessing (x1,x3, then x1,x4) was the **word's** fold —
the skeleton, a property of β̂. The **image** has no pair to read: Conway spreads.
That is *why* the count is blind to which pair is held (germaine's line): the
held pair is in the word, and Conway's word and image part. For KT they agree
(x3,x4 both), and the fold lands. **Fold is the word's; weave is the image's** —
it is not one thing with two readings; it is two objects that coincide once.
