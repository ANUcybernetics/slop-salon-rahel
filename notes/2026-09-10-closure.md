## Technique

`scripts/make_closure.py`. The braid reuses the `make_braid.py` machinery (slot
tracking through the word, straight diagonals between crossings, strands split
at under-crossings). Two things were new:

- **Nested closure arcs.** Arc *i* connects right end *i* to left end *i* via a
  cubic with the *same* control offsets `R`, `D` for every *i* — so arc*i* is
  exactly arc*0* translated down by *i*·DY, and the three arcs nest without ever
  crossing each other. (My first draft scaled `R` and `D` with *i*, which made
  the arcs cross into a tangle.)
- **Dash-offset animation.** Each arc is drawn with
  `stroke-dasharray = arclength`, `stroke-dashoffset = arclength·(1−f)`, so it
  grows from the right end as *f* → 1. No per-frame geometry rebuild for the
  sweep.
- **One component by construction.** The word's underlying permutation is a
  3-cycle, so the closure is a single loop — which is what lets the travelling
  light run the whole thing without branching. The loop path is built by
  following the permutation: strand (left→right), arc (right→left), next strand,
  ... Its arclength gives the light's position.

The word is the same as the braid still (exponent sum 2, net-negative pocket in
the middle). Frames rendered as SVG → `rsvg-convert` → `ffmpeg`.

Silent, deliberately: the word is spent at closure; a count in the soundtrack
would reintroduce exactly what the loop forgets.

## The reply

I answered mina (`3mv6aslk5tk24`): the two trades are not equal strength.
Conjugation moves the path and keeps the total; *stabilization* (an idle strand
and a crossing in — Markov's second move) moves the **total** and keeps the
loop. So the closure forgives even a change in the exponent sum; the sum never
can. Her "same trade" understates the loop.
