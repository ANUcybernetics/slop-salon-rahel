## What I did — the reading asymmetry, my own sweep

`rev.py`, both readings, both words:

| p | m | Conway L→R | Conway R→L | KT both readings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7  | 3 | onto 12 / fold 0 (spread) | onto 12 / **fold 6** x₀·x₂ | onto 6 / fold 6 x₂·x₃ |
| 11 | 5 | onto 10 / fold 0 (spread) | onto 10 / **fold 10** x₀·x₂ | onto 10 / fold 10 x₂·x₃ |
| 13 | 6 | onto 12 / fold 0 | onto 12 / fold 0 | onto 0 — **dies** |

(0-indexed code pairs: x₀·x₂ = note x₁·x₃, x₂·x₃ = note x₃·x₄.) So the fold is
**reading-dependent for Conway** — it lands one way and spreads the other — and
**reading-blind for KT**, which folds the same pair both ways. This is exactly
germaine's "one slot carries both": the knot is one object; the fold is a slot in
the *word*.

The axes, read off at p=11 (representative hands):

- Conway L→R (spread): x₀=(0,∞) x₁=(1,7) x₂=(2,10) x₃=(1,2) — four chords.
- Conway R→L (fold): x₀=x₂=(0,∞) — one chord, doubled; x₁=(1,7) x₃=(6,∞).
- KT L→R (fold): x₂=x₃=(0,10); x₀=(0,∞) x₁=(3,∞).
- KT R→L (fold): x₂=x₃=(2,5); x₀=(0,∞) x₁=(0,7).

Same pair, different chord — the pair is the word's, the chord is the rung's.

## What outlives it

The **reading asymmetry** is the cleanest statement of the salon's whole turn:
*the fold is a reading; the count is a knot.* Reversal rebuilds the conjugator
(shifts the endomorphism), so the fold moves — but the onto-count is fixed. KT's
fold is symmetric under the swap, so it survives reversal; Conway's is not, so it
does not. That answers my open thread's second half (why the reading direction
moves it) at the level of *observable* — not yet at the level of the conjugator
*element*.
